HTU has been invited by the Aqaba Development Corporation to take part in **ABOFA — Aqaba Blue: Ocean Future in Action**, an international exhibition for diving, marine tourism, and the blue economy, held alongside the **Regional Ocean Summit**.

## 3. The Five Official Tracks

The application form lists **5 tracks** (Marine Monitoring, Diving & Tourism, Circular Economy, AI & Ocean Science, Conservation Hardware), matching the promotional poster. The printed briefing packet, however, elaborates on **more than 5 challenge themes** (8 are referenced in the "Step 1" instructions, though only 6 are shown across the provided pages — likely 2 additional pages/themes exist beyond what was photographed). Below is everything captured from the packet.

### Track 1: Marine & Coastal Monitoring
**Framing:** *Real-time Ocean Intelligence — Data, sensors, and AI for ocean visibility*

- **Problem:** The ocean is vast and largely invisible. There's a lack of real-time data on ocean health, biodiversity, and emerging threats — creating blind spots for decision-makers, researchers, policymakers, and businesses.
- **Core question:** *How do we create comprehensive, real-time monitoring systems that give us visibility into ocean health?*
- **What you could build:** A monitoring platform · An AI data analysis system · A sensor network · An early warning system · A citizen science app
- **Consider:** What specific aspect of ocean monitoring would have the most impact — coral health? Fish populations? Pollution levels? Coastal erosion? Focus on one area where better data would enable better decisions.

### Track 2: Diving & Marine Tourism
**Framing:** *Conservation-Linked Tourism — Revenue generation + ecosystem protection*

- **Problem:** Marine tourism generates billions in revenue but often damages the ecosystems it depends on. Most tourism revenue doesn't benefit conservation — a misalignment between economic incentives and environmental protection.
- **Core question:** *How do we create tourism models where the more tourists visit, the more the ocean is protected?*
- **What you could build:** A certification system · A revenue-to-conservation platform · Virtual experiences · Community-based tourism · Impact monitoring
- **Consider:** How can you align economic incentives with conservation? What would make tourists willing to pay more for sustainable experiences? How do you measure and verify conservation impact?

### Track 3: Circular Blue Economy
**Framing:** *Regenerative Ocean Industries — Zero waste and circular supply chains*

- **Problem:** The ocean economy is still largely linear: extract resources, use them, discard waste. This creates pollution, resource depletion, and ecosystem damage. Regenerative models are needed where waste becomes input and industries actively restore ecosystems.
- **Core question:** *How do we build circular supply chains and regenerative practices in ocean-based industries?*
- **What you could build:** A circular supply chain platform · Ocean plastic recycling · Regenerative aquaculture · Seaweed farming · Waste-to-value solutions
- **Consider:** Which ocean industry has the most waste? How could that waste become valuable? What business model would make circular practices profitable? Focus on one industry or waste stream.

### Track 4: AI for Ocean Science
**Framing:** *Machine Learning for Marine Research — AI to understand and protect the ocean*

- **Problem:** Ocean science generates massive amounts of data — from satellites, sensors, research vessels, and underwater cameras — but analyzing it requires years of human research. AI can accelerate discovery, improve predictions, and enable real-time decision-making.
- **Core question:** *How do we apply AI and machine learning to solve critical ocean science problems?*
- **What you could build:** Species identification AI · Predictive ocean modeling · Fisheries management ML · Autonomous research systems · Data analysis platform
- **Consider:** What ocean science problem would benefit most from AI? What data is available? How would better predictions change decision-making? Start with a specific use case.

### Track 5: Marine Conservation Hardware & Software
**Framing:** *Technology for Marine Protection — Innovative tools for conservation at scale*

- **Problem:** Conservation happens at scale through technology and coordination. Better tools are needed for monitoring marine protected areas, preventing illegal fishing, restoring habitats, and coordinating conservation efforts across organizations and borders.
- **Core question:** *How do we build technology that scales marine conservation?*
- **What you could build:** Anti-poaching detection · Habitat restoration tech · MPA (Marine Protected Area) monitoring · Conservation coordination · Acoustic monitoring
- **Consider:** What is the biggest barrier to marine conservation — monitoring? Enforcement? Coordination? Funding? Focus on one barrier that technology can address.

## 4. Additional Challenge Themes (from the packet, beyond the 5 official tracks)

The briefing packet includes further challenge areas not listed as checkboxes on the application form itself, but presented as part of the "8 challenge areas" referenced in Step 1 of the approach guide. These may represent bonus/adjacent themes or sub-tracks:

### Smart Port Infrastructure
**Framing:** *Decarbonizing Maritime Logistics — Digital transformation of ports*

- **Problem:** Ports are critical infrastructure for global trade but are often inefficient and carbon-intensive. Digital transformation can optimize operations, reduce emissions, enable autonomous systems, and create more sustainable maritime logistics.
- **Core question:** *How do we modernize ports to be more efficient, sustainable, and innovative?*
- **What you could build:** Digital port management · Autonomous vessels · Cargo tracking · Logistics optimization · Port electrification
- **Consider:** What is the biggest inefficiency in port operations? How could digital technology improve it? What would reduce emissions? Focus on one operational challenge.

### Coastal Resilience & Adaptation
**Framing:** *Protecting Coastal Communities — Nature-based solutions for climate resilience*

- **Problem:** Coastal communities face rising seas, stronger storms, and ecosystem collapse. Traditional hard infrastructure is expensive and often ineffective. Nature-based solutions offer protection while restoring ecosystems and creating co-benefits for communities.
- **Core question:** *How do we build resilient coastal communities that adapt to climate change?*
- **What you could build:** Mangrove restoration · Artificial reefs · Nature-based protection · Adaptation planning · Ecosystem monitoring
- **Consider:** What coastal communities are most vulnerable? What nature-based solutions are most effective in their context? How can you finance and scale restoration? Focus on one region or solution type.

### Maritime Innovation & Sustainability
**Framing:** *Green Shipping Solutions — Sustainable maritime industries*

- **Problem:** The shipping industry is a major source of emissions and pollution. Transitioning to green technologies, alternative fuels, and sustainable practices is critical — including decarbonizing shipping, creating sustainable seafood supply chains, and managing marine waste.
- **Core question:** *How do we make maritime industries sustainable?*
- **What you could build:** Green fuel systems · Sustainable seafood platforms · Waste collection · Ocean cleanup · Emissions monitoring
- **Consider:** What is the biggest sustainability challenge in maritime industries — emissions? Pollution? Overfishing? Waste? Focus on one challenge where your solution can have measurable impact.

## 5. How to Approach Your Challenge (Official 6-Step Method)

The packet lays out a required structuring method: *"The key to a successful hackathon solution is focus. Don't try to solve the entire ocean economy. Instead, pick one specific challenge, understand it deeply, and build a focused MVP that proves your concept."*

1. **Choose Your Track** — Select one of the challenge areas that resonates with you. Each represents a critical opportunity in the regenerative ocean economy. Consider your team's skills and interests.
2. **Understand the Problem** — Read the challenge description carefully. Understand what the problem is, who it affects, and why it matters. Ask: *Why does this problem exist? What's preventing it from being solved today?*
3. **Define Your Scope** — Be specific. Don't try to solve everything. Focus on one aspect of the challenge addressable in three days. Write a clear problem statement defining exactly what you're solving.
4. **Identify Your Approach** — How will you solve it — technology? Business model? Policy? Community engagement? What makes your approach unique? Why will your solution work when others haven't?
5. **Design Your MVP** — What's the minimum viable product you can build in three days? Focus on proving your core concept, not building everything. Your MVP should demonstrate the key insight or innovation.
6. **Connect to Impact** — How will you measure success? What environmental, economic, or social impact will your solution create? Be specific and measurable. Connect your solution to the $550B opportunity.

> **Note on timeline discrepancy:** The approach guide repeatedly references a "three day" build window ("addressable in three days," "minimum viable product you can build in three days"), while the official dates list a two-week hackathon (30 Jul–13 Aug). This may indicate the packet template was reused from a different (3-day) hackathon format, or that judging/demo prep is expected to be compressed into an intensive final 3-day sprint within the 2-week window. Worth clarifying with organizers if it affects your planning.
