# 11 · Business — Market sizing

**Part C. How big is this, and how do we know?**

> **The short version.** **~12 sites we could actually serve.** That is the honest number, and it
> is small. It is still the right market, for four reasons: the buyers are wealthy and
> concentrated, contracts are sticky, the cost base is tiny, and the same engine serves much
> larger adjacent markets (dredging, brine, spills, algal blooms).
>
> **Realistic revenue:** **$0–80k** in year one (grant-funded, not sold) → **$450k–1.5M** by year
> three. **This is a grant-to-contract business, not a venture-scale SaaS.**
>
> **MENA is not a segment of this — it is nearly all of it.** **9 of the 12 sites**, all six
> non-Caribbean Tier 1 sites, **~$2.2 bn/yr** of reef tourism value at risk, and a steady-state
> ceiling of **~$750k–1.25M/yr**. See **§2A**.
>
> **⚠️ The trap:** reef-valuation figures disagree by **two orders of magnitude**. Use the
> conservative direct-use number (**$112k/km²/yr**), never Costanza's ~$35M/km². Applying Costanza
> gives "$200 million of reef," which collapses under one question and takes your honest numbers
> down with it.
>
> **The defensible line:** the Aqaba Marine Reserve is worth **~$0.25–1.5 M/yr**, Jordan's whole
> reef **~$0.6–3.8 M/yr** — protected by a system costing tens of thousands.

Every number below is either **[sourced]** with a citation or **[assumption]** with the
arithmetic shown. Nothing is asserted without one of the two. A judge who asks "where did
that number come from?" should be able to follow it in under thirty seconds.

---

## 0 · The honest framing first

Coral reef valuation literature disagrees with itself by **two orders of magnitude**, and
you need to know that before quoting any of it:

| Source | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| UNEP | **US$100,000 – 600,000 per km²/yr** **[sourced]** | Widely cited planning range |
| Asia-Pacific direct use (fisheries + tourism) | **US$112,000 per km²/yr**, range $550 (Tuvalu) → $795,000 (Guam) **[sourced]** | Measured direct use only |
| Costanza et al. 2014 | **~US$350,000 per hectare/yr** = **~$35 million per km²/yr** **[sourced]** | ~60× the UNEP midpoint |

**[judgement]** Use the **UNEP range or the Asia-Pacific direct-use figure**. The Costanza
number includes non-market ecosystem services under a methodology that is actively contested,
and quoting $35M/km² will invite an argument you cannot win in a two-minute Q&A. If someone
challenges you on it, the strong answer is: *"we deliberately used the conservative
direct-use figure."*

## 1 · TAM — the whole problem class

### Top-down

- Global coral reef tourism: **~US$36 billion/yr, ~70 million visitor trips, 100+ countries
  and territories** ([Spalding et al. 2017](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0308597X17300635))
  **[sourced]**
- **More than one-quarter of the world's reefs are threatened by watershed-based pollution;
  about 10% highly threatened** ([WRI](https://www.wri.org/data/reefs-risk-watershed-based-pollution))
  **[sourced]**

**[derived]** If reef tourism value is even roughly proportional to reef area, the reefs
under watershed-based pollution threat sit under **~25% of $36 bn ≈ $9 billion/yr of reef
tourism value**. That is the economic activity exposed to the problem class ReefShield
addresses — *not* a revenue target, and it must never be presented as one.

### Why the arid subset is much smaller — and say so

**[sourced]** Ningaloo's World Heritage nomination notes that its arid setting
*"distinguishes it from most reefs in the world, which are found associated with wet
tropical rainforest habitat."* Most of that $9 bn sits on wet tropical coasts already served
by watershed-management programmes.

**[judgement]** ReefShield's TAM is the **arid and semi-arid** subset. There is no published
figure for it. Two honest ways to bound it:

1. **Red Sea alone holds ~6% of global coral reefs** **[sourced]**, and virtually all of it
   is arid coast. 6% of $36 bn ≈ **$2.2 bn/yr of reef tourism value in the Red Sea system**.
2. Add the Gulf of Oman, the semi-arid Caribbean, SW Madagascar and Baja — all identified in
   Part B — and a **$3–4 bn/yr exposed tourism value** is a defensible order of magnitude
   **[assumption, arithmetic above]**.

> **Do not claim a TAM in software revenue terms from these numbers.** They measure the
> value *at risk*, which is the justification for spending, not the spend itself. The
> software market is derived separately in §3.

### Value at risk — MENA

**[derived]** From the same arithmetic as §1, restricted to the region:

| Layer | Figure | Basis |
|---|---|---|

### Realistic MENA revenue

**[assumption]** Two views, because they answer different questions.

**Three-year view** — take the §3 build-out and note that the sites in it are almost entirely
MENA: Aqaba (yr 1), Egypt corridor + a giga-project pilot (yr 2), Oman + Saudi expansion (yr 3).
Only Bonaire is not. So **~85–90% of the year-three $450k–1.5M is MENA → ~$400k–1.35M/yr.**

**Full-build view** — all 9 MENA sites live, which is a year 4+ scenario, using the
per-site contribution from [`13-economics.md`](13-economics.md) §4:

| Class | Sites | Contribution each | Subtotal |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Authority-class (ASEZA, Egypt EEAA, Oman MECA, Fujairah, Palestine INPA) | 7 | ~$73k/yr | **~$511k** |
| Giga-project class (NEOM/Saudi Aqaba coast, Jeddah) | 2 | $150–400k licence − ~$29k cost = $121–371k | **~$242–742k** |
| **MENA steady state** | **9** | | **~$750k – 1.25M/yr** |

**[judgement]** That is the honest ceiling for a MENA-only version of this business: **under
$1.5M/yr, sustaining a team of roughly four to six.** It does not become a large company by
adding MENA sites — it becomes one only via the adjacent industrial markets in §4.4.

### The number that actually decides the MENA figure

**[judgement]** Not the site count — **the Saudi giga-projects.** NEOM, Red Sea Global and
Jeddah Central sit inside a **$1.3 trillion** programme **[sourced]**, and a single
environmental-compliance contract there at the top of the $150–400k band is worth **five
authority-class sites**. The MENA number is therefore bimodal: without a giga-project it is
~$500k/yr; with one it is ~$900k/yr; with two it approaches $1.25M.

**The same fact is the risk.** One contract at $400k would be **~30–50% of all MENA revenue** —
the customer-concentration risk already logged in [`13-economics.md`](13-economics.md) §5.
Worth stating before a judge does.

### The valuation trap, MENA edition

**[judgement]** §0's warning gets *more* dangerous at regional scale, not less. Applying
Costanza's ~$35M/km² to Red Sea reef area produces a **multi-billion-dollar** "value protected"
headline. It is indefensible under one question and it would discredit the $2.2 bn figure —
which is itself sourced and defensible — sitting right next to it. **Use the direct-use number.**

## 3 · SOM — realistic revenue, three years

This section is **[assumption]** throughout. The assumptions are stated so they can be
argued with, replaced, or defended.

### Pricing assumptions

No public comparable exists for "arid-coast reef sediment forecasting," because the product
does not exist. Anchors used:

| Buyer class | Assumed annual contract | Basis |
|---|---:|---|
| National environmental agency / marine park authority | **$40k – 120k** | Mid-range government environmental SaaS **[assumption]** |
| Mega-development / giga-project (NEOM, Red Sea Global) | **$150k – 400k** | Environmental compliance line item inside a multi-billion programme **[assumption]** |
| Donor/NGO-funded deployment (Madagascar) | **$0 – 50k** | Grant-funded, cost-recovery only **[assumption]** |
| Dive-operator association (aggregated) | **$10k – 30k** | Too small individually; sold via association **[assumption]** |

### Three-year build-out

| Year | Sites live | Mix | Revenue range **[assumption]** |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 1 | 1–2 | Aqaba (ASEZA), pilot-funded | **$0 – 80k** — realistically grant-funded, not sold |
| 2 | 3–4 | + Egypt corridor, + one giga-project pilot | **$150k – 500k** |
| 3 | 6–8 | + Oman, + Saudi expansion, + Bonaire | **$450k – 1.5M** |

**[judgement]** This is a **grant-to-contract business, not a venture-scale SaaS**. The
serviceable market is a dozen institutional buyers, and most of them procure through public
tender or donor programme rather than a credit card. Anyone modelling it as a
product-led-growth SaaS will be wrong.

**The right comparison is a specialist environmental-intelligence consultancy with a
software product**, not a startup. Which is exactly what the funding landscape in
[`14-funding.md`](14-funding.md) is built for.

## 4 · Why a small market is still the right market

**[judgement]** Four reasons this is a good business despite twelve sites:

1. **The buyers are wealthy and concentrated.** NEOM, Red Sea Global and Jeddah Central sit
   inside a **$1.3 trillion** Saudi programme **[sourced]**. One environmental compliance
   contract there exceeds the entire rest of the pipeline.
2. **Contracts are sticky.** Once reef zone IDs and catchment IDs are embedded in an
   agency's reporting, switching means invalidating the historical record. The ID contract
   in [`tasks/00-contracts.md`](../../../tasks/00-contracts.md) is accidentally also a
   commercial moat **[judgement]**.
3. **The cost base is tiny.** The platform runs on free open data — see
   [`17-scaling.md`](17-scaling.md). Gross margin is
   structurally high because there is no data licensing cost.
4. **It is adjacent to much larger markets.** The same particle engine and exposure scoring
   serve **dredge plumes, desalination brine, oil spills, wastewater and harmful algal
   blooms** — already listed in the concept doc §26 Phase 5. Those markets are large,
   industrial, and regulated. Sediment forecasting is the wedge, not the ceiling.

## 5 · The value-at-risk calculation for Aqaba specifically

This is the number to put in front of ASEZA. **[derived]**

### The authoritative geography **[sourced]**

From the GFCR Gulf of Aqaba concept note and the AMR management plan:

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Gulf of Aqaba | semi-enclosed basin, **~180 km long, 14–26 km wide**, average depth **800 m** |
| Coastline by country | **Egypt ~180 km · Palestine ~9 km · Jordan ~27 km · Saudi Arabia ~150 km** |
| Jordan's fringing reef | runs **over 25 km** of the 27 km coast |
| **Aqaba Marine Reserve** | **2.45 km², covering 7 km of the 27 km coastline** — about **2.6% of Jordan's marine area** |
| Dive sites monitored/partly managed by the park | **19** |

> **Correction to the project's own docs:** [`tasks/nizar.md`](../../../tasks/nizar.md) states
> the gulf is "~15–25 km wide". The authoritative figure is **14–26 km**. Immaterial to the
> argument — the resolution problem is identical — but worth fixing before it reaches a
> slide.

### Two different reef areas — do not conflate them

**[derived]** There is a designated reserve and there is a reef. They are not the same size:

- **Aqaba Marine Reserve: 2.45 km²** **[sourced]** — the legally protected area, 7 km of coast.
- **Jordan's full fringing reef:** >25 km of coast × ~250 m band **[assumption, matching
  Pulga's documented 250 m assumption in [`docs/pitch_limitations.md`](../../pitch_limitations.md) §4]**
  → **~6.25 km²**. Cross-check: the committed `reef_zones_PROVISIONAL.gpkg` totals **5.69 km²**
  across 8 zones ([`docs/README_pulga.md`](../../README_pulga.md)) **[sourced]** — within 10%,
  so the assumption is not wild.

**Applying the sourced value ranges:**

| Basis | AMR only (2.45 km²) | Full Jordanian reef (~5.7–6.25 km²) |
|---|---|---|
| Asia-Pacific direct use, $112k/km² | **~$0.27 M/yr** | **~$0.6 – 0.7 M/yr** |
| UNEP low, $100k/km² | **~$0.25 M/yr** | **~$0.6 M/yr** |
| UNEP high, $600k/km² | **~$1.5 M/yr** | **~$3.4 – 3.8 M/yr** |

**[judgement]** So the protected reserve is plausibly worth **$0.25–1.5 million/yr** and
Jordan's whole reef **$0.6–3.8 million/yr** in direct-use terms. Quote whichever matches the
audience — but always say which one you are quoting. That is the honest range, and it makes
the pitch *sharper*, not weaker:

> A system costing tens of thousands a year protects an asset generating low single-digit
> millions a year — and unlike the reef, the system is replaceable.

**Resist the temptation to inflate this.** Applying Costanza's $35M/km² would give
"$200 million of reef," which is indefensible under questioning and would undermine every
other number in the deck.

## 6 · What would sharpen these numbers

**[judgement]** In priority order:

1. **Aqaba diver-day counts and Marine Reserve entrance-fee revenue.** ASEZA holds these.
   The [Aqaba Marine Reserve Management Plan 2022–2026](https://www.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/2023-03/AMR%20Managemnt%20Plan%202022-2026.pdf)
   is the likely public source — **that PDF blocks automated fetching, so somebody needs to
   open it in a browser.** Area (2.45 km²), coastline (7 km) and dive-site count (19) have
   since been recovered from secondary sources; **visitor numbers and budget have not**. This
   remains the highest-value missing number in the business case.
2. **Cost of a single reef damage event** — survey costs, dive-site closure days, remediation.
   Converts the pitch from "protects value" to "avoids cost."
3. **ASEZA's environment directorate budget.** Sets the realistic contract ceiling for the
   home market.
