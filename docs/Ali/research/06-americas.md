# 06 · Rest of World — The Americas

**Part B. Bonaire and Curaçao, Baja California Sur, Puerto Rico and the USVI.**

> **The short version.** **Bonaire / Curaçao (11/12) is the best non-MENA commercial target** — a
> dive-tourism economy exactly like Aqaba's, ephemeral *rooien*, a funded marine park, and the

> generalised the solution.

## Site 15 · Bonaire and Curaçao (ABC islands) — Score 11/12

**The closest non-MENA analogue in the world, and the one with the most transferable
customer.**

| Criterion | Score | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| C1 ephemeral | 2 | Semi-arid islands drained by *rooien* — ephemeral gullies that run only after storms **[sourced]** |
| C2 intense episodic | 2 | Damage is driven by **extreme rainfall events**, not baseline conditions **[sourced]** |
| C3 reef proximity | 2 | Fringing reef metres from shore around both islands **[sourced]** |
| C4 confined basin | 1 | Open Caribbean, but the leeward coast retains material |
| C5 development | 2 | Tourism development, Willemstad, refinery; **coastal development such as sewage discharge and artificial beach construction likely caused the decline of shallow reefs** on Curaçao **[sourced]** |
| C6 data-poor | 2 | Research institute presence (CARMABI), but no operational forecasting |

### The evidence

- **In Bonaire, an arid climate means deep-rooted trees are less effective than herbaceous
  species at arresting shallow topsoil runoff. Major land-use alteration at steep, highly
  erodible semi-arid islands accelerates soil erosion, runoff, and sedimentation stress to
  nearshore coral reefs during extreme rainfall events.** **[sourced]** — this is a
  near-verbatim restatement of ReefShield's own problem statement, from the Caribbean.
- Land-based pressures on Bonaire's marine environment include **wastewater, stormwater
  runoff, erosion and other pollution entering the sea and degrading water quality**
  ([Caribbean Compass](https://caribbeancompass.com/alarms-sounded-over-water-quality-in-bonaires-coastal-waters/))
  **[sourced]**.
- Bonaire is cited as a **case study for a watershed strategy for stormwater and sewage
  management** ([Reef Resilience Network](https://reefresilience.org/managing-local-threats/reducing-land-based-impacts/))
  **[sourced]**.
- Recent work maps **flow patterns, hotspots and connectivity of land-derived substances at
  the sea surface of Curaçao** ([Ocean Science, 2025](https://os.copernicus.org/articles/21/945/2025/))
  **[sourced]** — i.e. someone is actively working the marine-transport half here. Worth
  reading before claiming novelty in this specific location.

### Why the customer transfers so well

**[judgement]** Bonaire's economy is **dive tourism**, in the same way Aqaba's is. The buyer
persona from [`reefshield_aqaba_concept.md`](../../../reefshield_aqaba_concept.md) §7.4 — a
dive centre wanting an inspection or temporary-closure window — exists here in larger
numbers and with more purchasing power. Bonaire National Marine Park is a well-established,
funded manager. Of every site outside MENA, **this is the one where the product could be
sold essentially unchanged.**

## Site 16 · Baja California Sur, Mexico — Score 9/12

| Criterion | Score | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| C1 ephemeral | 2 | Arroyos; **the Santiago River runs sporadically into the Gulf after chubascos and hurricanes, depositing large pulses of sandy granitic sediment into the sea** **[sourced]** |
| C2 intense episodic | 2 | **Since 1996, as many as eight major hurricanes entered the Gulf of California**, most crossing near Isla Cerralvo/La Paz **[sourced]** |
| C3 reef proximity | 1 | **Cabo Pulmo** — the largest coral reserve in the Gulf of California, a 20,000-year-old reef **[sourced]** — but not directly at an arroyo mouth the way Aqaba's reefs sit at wadi outlets |
| C4 confined basin | 1 | Gulf of California is semi-enclosed but ~150 km wide — an order of magnitude less confined than the Gulf of Aqaba, so plume dilution is far greater **[judgement]** |
| C5 development | 1 | Los Cabos tourism corridor; La Paz. Cabo Pulmo itself is deliberately undeveloped |
| C6 data-poor | 2 | Strong marine biology presence in La Paz; no operational land-to-sea system found |

**The Cabo Pulmo story is a double-edged asset [judgement].** It is one of conservation's
great success stories — a fully protected no-take area whose fish biomass recovered
dramatically — which makes it *highly defended politically* and a compelling narrative. But
the threats named in the literature are **sewage and wastewater runoff driving algal
growth** and **microplastics in sediment beds** **[sourced]** — nutrient and contaminant
framing more than sediment framing. The product would lead with pollutant transport rather
than turbidity.

**Hurricane-driven, like Oman.** Same benefit (days of lead time), same complication (wave
damage confounds attribution).

## Site 17 · Puerto Rico (Guánica) and USVI (Coral Bay) — Score 9/12

**Fits the signature, but is the most *already-served* site in the scan.**

| Criterion | Score | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| C1 ephemeral | 1 | Dry southwestern PR; seasonal streams |
| C2 intense episodic | 2 | **Suspended sediment from unpaved roads documented after brief rain showers in Coral Bay, St. John** **[sourced]** |
| C3 reef proximity | 2 | Nearshore reef habitat directly downstream of the watersheds **[sourced]** |
| C4 confined basin | 2 | Guánica Bay, Coral Bay, Fish Bay |
| C5 development | 2 | Agriculture, unpaved roads, settlement |
| C6 data-poor | **0** | **NOAA already runs a near-real-time ocean-colour water-clarity product here** — see below |

### The striking number

**Road erosion rates were 330–760× greater than from undisturbed hillslopes**, depending on
slope and grading; road infiltration was **4–11 mm/h, only 10–25% of undisturbed hillslope
rates**, causing roads to generate runoff **~5× more frequently**
([Journal of Soils and Sediments](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11368-024-03749-2))
**[sourced, corroborated across independent summaries; full text paywalled]**.

> **Location precision.** This study is from **Isla de Culebra**, an offshore island of
> Puerto Rico — **not** the Guánica Bay watershed on the main island. The two are grouped
> here as one Caribbean dry-setting site, but do not present the Culebra erosion figures as
> Guánica measurements. The authors note that **no empirical data documenting unpaved-road
> sediment production existed for Culebra before this work** **[sourced]**.

**[judgement]** This is the best single statistic in the scan for the argument that
**land-surface condition, not just rainfall, controls sediment delivery** — which is exactly
what Component C's land-cover and soil features encode. Use it to justify why the runoff
model needs Pulga's WorldCover/SoilGrids layers and not just rainfall.

### Why it scores 0 on C6

NOAA Coral Reef Watch lists **"Chlorophyll-*a* and Kd(490) monitoring at 750 m from VIIRS
satellite (Hawaii and Puerto Rico)"** at **experimental status, v1.0**
([NOAA CRW Product Overview](https://coralreefwatch.noaa.gov/satellite/product_overview.php))
**[sourced, verified verbatim]**. Kd(490) is a diffuse-attenuation water-clarity proxy; the
product exists to help managers monitor conditions after large rain events. Guánica Bay
also has a **funded, multi-agency restoration programme** with explicit sediment-reduction
goals ([NOAA Digital Coast](https://coast.noaa.gov/digitalcoast/stories/guanica-bay.html))
**[sourced]**.

**So Puerto Rico is not a market — it is the proof that the market is real.** A US federal
agency built a dedicated ocean-colour product for exactly this management need, for exactly
two regions on Earth, and it is still experimental. The need is recognised; nobody has
generalised the solution **[judgement]**.
