---

## Why this region is a different variant

In the Gulf of Aqaba the rainfall driver is a **synoptic/convective** system over a
hyper-arid desert. On the Arabian Sea and Gulf of Oman coasts, the driver is often a
**tropical cyclone**. The land-to-sea consequence is the same — a sediment pulse onto
nearshore reef — but three things change:

1. **Predictability improves.** Cyclones are tracked days ahead. Lead time goes from hours
   to days, which makes the *forecast* mode far more valuable **[judgement]**.
2. **Mechanical damage confounds the signal.** Cyclones break coral with waves as well as
   smothering it with sediment. Attribution is harder **[judgement]**.
3. **Freshwater shock joins sediment as a stressor** — and this is documented, not
   speculative (below).

## Site 10 · Muscat and the Daymaniyat Islands, Oman — Score 11/12

**The best-documented cyclone variant, with explicit reef damage attributed to
sedimentation and freshwater.**

| Criterion | Score | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| C1 ephemeral | 2 | Wadi Aday and the Muscat wadi systems; severe wadi flash floods have recurred repeatedly with major impact on lives and property **[sourced]** |
| C2 intense episodic | 2 | **Cyclone Gonu, June 2007** — the strongest tropical cyclone on record in the Arabian Sea. Casualty and loss figures **vary by source**: roughly **49–50 deaths and US$4–4.2 billion** in damage in Oman **[sourced, range]** |
| C3 reef proximity | 2 | Coral thrives near Muscat, the **Daymaniyat Islands**, Musandam and Bar al Hikman **[sourced]** |
| C4 confined basin | 1 | Gulf of Oman is open, but the Capital Area coast retains material |
| C5 development | 2 | Muscat metropolitan area, Mina Al Fahal port/refinery |
| C6 data-poor | 2 | Flood hazard GIS work exists; no marine forecasting found |

### The key finding

> Cyclone Gonu's impacts were significant on mainland sites around the Capital Area, where
> corals were affected **not only by wave-induced mechanical damage but were also impacted
> by freshwater exposure resulting from heavy rains, as well as sedimentation from wadis
> discharging into the sea.**
>
> — *Oman's coral reefs: a unique ecosystem challenged by natural and man-related stresses*
> ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/284123597_Oman's_coral_reefs_A_unique_ecosystem_challenged_by_natural_and_man-related_stresses_and_in_need_of_conservation))
> **[sourced]**

**This is the single best piece of external validation in the entire scan.** It is a
published, independent statement of ReefShield's exact causal chain — *wadis discharging
into the sea → sedimentation → coral damage* — from a different country, a different ocean
basin and a different rainfall driver **[judgement]**.

**Attribution note:** an earlier draft of this document credited this passage to *Twenty-year
changes in coral near Muscat, Oman* (Marine Environmental Research). That was wrong; that
paper covers the manta-tow trend analysis. The wadi/sedimentation statement is from the
Oman coral reefs review cited above. Corrected 2026-08-02.

**Scale of the Gonu impact [sourced]:** a **two-thirds decline in coral cover at the
Daymaniyat Islands**, and **up to 99% coral mortality at wave-exposed locations**. Note that
those two figures are **wave-driven**, which is precisely the attribution problem flagged
above — do not present them as sediment damage.

Oman's corals additionally face **relatively high background turbidity from high ocean
phytoplankton productivity** **[sourced]** — which matters technically: Abd's spectral
plume-detection approach would need recalibration, because the "normal water" baseline is
not clear blue water. **[judgement]**

### Also in Oman: Dhofar / Salalah — Score 9/12

Wadi floods impacted Dhofar during the **2002 cyclonic storm**, with impact assessments run
under present and future sea-level conditions
([Journal of Arid Environments](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0140196318304397))
**[sourced]**. Dhofar has the added complexity of the **khareef** monsoon, which makes it
seasonally green — a different hydrology from hyper-arid Aqaba **[judgement]**.

## Site 11 · Fujairah and the UAE east coast — Score 10/12

| Criterion | Score | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| C1 ephemeral | 2 | **Intermittent wadi systems discharge from the mountains to the coast, affecting nearshore sedimentation and water quality, especially after episodic rainfall events** ([Coasts, MDPI](https://doi.org/10.3390/coasts6020022)) **[sourced]** |
| C2 intense episodic | 2 | Occasional heavy rainfall and flooding; a dam was built across Wadi Tawiyean in 1992 specifically to mitigate flash-flood danger **[sourced]** |
| C3 reef proximity | 1 | Coral reef patches, rocky reefs, seagrass beds and sandy subtidal habitats nearshore **[sourced]** — but patchy, see the caveat below |
| C4 confined basin | 1 | Gulf of Oman, open but sheltered by headlands |
| C5 development | 2 | Fujairah port — one of the world's largest bunkering hubs — plus coastal development |
| C6 data-poor | 2 | Coastal vulnerability mapping exists; no operational marine forecasting found |

**The direct statement [sourced]:** *sedimentation particularly affected the nearshore reefs,
and this impact has been exacerbated by coastal development activities.*

**One honest caveat [sourced]:** the Gulf of Oman is described in the regional literature as
**sedimentary and exposed, therefore unsuitable for coral growth**, though coral areas do
exist. So the reef resource is patchier and less charismatic than the Red Sea's. That
lowers the tourism value but does not remove the problem — and the **UAE's regulatory
appetite and budget for coastal environmental monitoring is high** **[judgement]**.

## Site 12 · Iranian Gulf of Oman / Makran coast — Score 7/12

Chabahar and the Makran coast are arid, wadi-drained, and subject to Arabian Sea cyclones.
Reef development is limited compared with the Red Sea, and searchable published evidence
linking flash-flood sediment to reef condition here is thin **[judgement]**. Listed for
regional completeness only; **do not build a slide around it without further reading.**

## The Arabian/Persian Gulf proper — mostly *not* a fit

**[judgement]** Bahrain, Qatar, and the UAE's western coast have coral reefs and arid
climates, but:

- Rainfall is lower and flash-flood generation weaker than on the mountainous east coast.
- The dominant reef stressors there are **extreme thermal stress and hypersalinity**, not
  terrestrial sediment.
- Dredging and reclamation are the main sediment sources — a *chronic, human-scheduled*
  input, not an episodic weather-driven one.

A dredge-plume forecasting product is an adjacent opportunity the architecture could serve
(the concept doc §26 Phase 5 already lists dredging sediment as a future extension), but
**it is a different trigger and should not be sold as the same product** **[judgement]**.

## What changes for the cyclone variant

| Component | Change needed | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| Rainfall forecast (Nizar's GFS/GEFS) | **Improves** — cyclone tracks give days of lead time instead of hours | None; benefit |
| Event detection thresholds | Recalibrate — cyclone rainfall totals dwarf convective ones | Low |
| Plume detection (Abd) | **Recalibrate baseline** — Oman's naturally high productivity/turbidity breaks the "clear water = normal" assumption | Medium |
| Exposure engine | Add a **wave/mechanical damage** term, or explicitly scope it out | Medium |
| Currents | Copernicus 1/12° in an open gulf — same coarseness caveat | None |

**[judgement]** Oman is the single most attractive non-Red-Sea MENA target: documented reef
damage from exactly this mechanism, a government with cyclone-response infrastructure
already in place, and a marine reserve (Daymaniyat) with a named manager.
