## The regional physical setting

Red Sea coastal sediment has two sources: **aeolian quartz from adjacent deserts**, and
**fluvial material derived from numerous wadis during flood discharge**
([PERSGA regional status report](https://archive.iwlearn.net/persga.org/Documents/Coral_reef_status_RSGA_2000_1_.pdf))
**[sourced]**. The second source is the one ReefShield forecasts.

The Red Sea holds the **world's fourth-largest barrier reef system and about 6% of global
coral reefs** ([AGBI](https://www.agbi.com/analysis/sustainability/2024/12/red-sea-coral-to-get-extra-protection-from-tourists/))
**[sourced]**.

**The important nuance, and it cuts both ways [sourced]:** Red Sea reefs *generally* receive
minimal terrestrial runoff or riverine input — that is part of why they are healthy — **but
terrestrial runoff degrades local coral reefs and contributes to increased severity and
prevalence of coral disease** where it does occur.

**[judgement]** This is the correct way to frame the problem in the Red Sea, and it is
*more* compelling than "the reefs are drowning in sediment":

> These reefs are adapted to almost no terrestrial input. That is exactly why a single
> flash-flood pulse is a shock rather than a background condition — and why episodic events
> deserve event-scale forecasting rather than annual water-quality averages.

## Site 4 · Egyptian Red Sea — Hurghada, Safaga, Marsa Alam — Score 11/12

**Egypt's dive tourism spine, with documented flood sediment on the reefs.**

| Criterion | Score | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| C1 ephemeral | 2 | Eastern Desert wadi systems; extensive flash-flood/recharge literature **[sourced]** |
| C2 intense episodic | 2 | **9 March 2014**, Wadi Billi flash flood extended to the Red Sea, damaging people and infrastructure ([Floodwater harvesting within Wadi Billi](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/23570008.2022.2129150)) **[sourced]** |
| C3 reef proximity | 2 | Marsa Alam: semi-closed sheltered lagoons and nearshore embayments with dense seagrass and diverse coral communities **[sourced]** |
| C4 confined basin | 1 | Open Red Sea coast, but sheltered lagoons and embayments retain material **[sourced]** |
| C5 development | 2 | Hurghada/Marsa Alam resort corridor, ports, coastal highway |
| C6 data-poor | 2 | Flash-flood hazard mapping exists; no marine forecasting **[sourced]** |

**The direct evidence.** At investigated Marsa Alam sites, the seafloor showed
**heterogeneous sediment layers resulting from terrestrial flash floods that deposited
large amounts of terrigenous sediments and coral reef fragments** **[sourced]**. More
broadly, **high quantities of flood sediment can impact coral reefs and promote hyperpycnal
flows**, and Eastern Desert flash floods reaching the Red Sea affect infrastructure and the
marine environment **[sourced]**.

**Economic exposure.** Coastal tourism contributes **over 3.5% of Egyptian GDP**; recreational
reef-related expenditure in Egypt was estimated at **$472 million (2000)**
([MEEA](https://meea.sites.luc.edu/volume14/PDFS/MEEA%202011%20Coral%20reefs%20and%20tourism%20in%20Egypts%20Red%20Sea%2016%20mai%202011_3.pdf))
**[sourced]**. Egypt's Red Sea is a premier diving destination attracting millions of
tourists **[sourced]**.

**[judgement]** This is the largest single addressable market in the scan: highest reef
tourism volume, documented flood-sediment deposition on reefs, and a dive industry with
direct commercial incentive to know when visibility will drop.

## Site 5 · Jeddah and the central Saudi Red Sea — Score 10/12

| Criterion | Score | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| C1 ephemeral | 2 | Hijaz escarpment wadis |
| C2 intense episodic | 2 | Severe urban flash flooding is a documented recurring hazard; 2024 torrential rains caused widespread regional disruption **[sourced]** |
| C3 reef proximity | 1 | Reefs present but generally further offshore than in the Gulf of Aqaba |
| C4 confined basin | 1 | Open coast, but Sharm Obhur and similar embayments are confined |
| C5 development | 2 | Jeddah metropolitan area; **Jeddah Central** giga-project **[sourced]** |
| C6 data-poor | 2 | Urban flood modelling exists; no marine continuation found |

**[judgement]** Jeddah is the strongest *urban-drainage* variant of the problem — the
sediment story is mixed with a wastewater and contaminant story. That is a different
emphasis (Component D's sediment-load proxy becomes a pollutant-load proxy) but the same
architecture.

**Red Sea Global**, the giga-project developing the Saudi central Red Sea coast for
tourism, has explicit reef-protection commitments **[sourced]** — a plausible commercial
counterpart.

## Site 6 · Sudan — Port Sudan, Dungonab Bay, Sanganeb — Score 10/12

| Criterion | Score | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| C1 ephemeral | 2 | Arid coast, khor/wadi drainage |
| C2 intense episodic | 1 | Red Sea Hills receive seasonal intense rainfall, but I found **no documented flash-flood-to-reef event** for this coast — scored down for absence of evidence, not evidence of absence **[judgement]** |
| C3 reef proximity | 2 | **Dungonab Bay and Mukkawar Island**, 125 km north of Port Sudan, is a UNESCO World Heritage property with a highly diverse system of coral reefs, mangroves, seagrass beds, beaches and islets ([UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/262/)) **[sourced]** |
| C4 confined basin | 2 | Dungonab Bay is a genuine embayment |
| C5 development | 1 | Port Sudan is a major port; the wider coast is undeveloped |
| C6 data-poor | 2 | Among the most data-poor reef coasts anywhere |

Sudanese reefs are **among the most pristine in the central Red Sea** **[sourced]**, and
Sudanese researchers from Red Sea University in Port Sudan already participate in
multidisciplinary Red Sea surveys alongside the Aqaba and Eilat institutes **[sourced]**.

**[judgement]** High scientific and conservation value, real existing research links to
Aqaba — but **conflict and access risk make this a research-partnership target, not a
near-term commercial one.** Listed for completeness and because a pristine baseline is
scientifically valuable, not because it is sellable in 2026.

## Sites 7–9 · Eritrea, Djibouti, Yemen/Socotra — Score 7–8/12

Grouped because the evidence base is thinner and the constraint is the same.

- **Djibouti** records 166–167 coral species, dominated by *Acropora hemprichi*,
  *Echinophora fruticulosa* and *Porites nodifera* **[sourced]**; Khor Ambado is a named
  coastal water body **[sourced]**. Arid, wadi-drained, reef-lined.
- **Socotra (Yemen)** appears in regional reef surveys alongside Red Sea sites **[sourced]**;
  it is arid, isolated, and biologically exceptional.
- **Eritrea (Massawa)** shares the southern Red Sea arid-coast setting **[sourced]**.

**[judgement]** All three satisfy the physical signature. All three are constrained by
conflict, access, or institutional capacity rather than by technical fit. They belong in a
"regional coverage" slide, not a commercial pipeline.

## Cross-cutting: what changes when ReefShield moves along the Red Sea

**[judgement]** Ordered by effort:

| Change | Effort | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| New AOI + catchment delineation | **Medium, not low** | Mahdi's `03_dem_fetch.py` → `06_catchments.py` chain runs anywhere in principle, but it is **not** parameter-driven: `01_make_aoi.py` hard-codes `MARINE_BBOX`, and `06_catchments.py` hard-codes `country == "Jordan"` plus `JORDAN_MAX_DIST_M`. Porting to Egypt means **editing source**, not swapping a config **[verified on `main`, 2026-08-02: `scripts/06_catchments.py:93` and `scripts/01_make_aoi.py:29`]** |
| IMERG / ERA5 ingestion | **None** | Already event- and box-agnostic by design **[sourced: `docs/pipeline_capability_report.md` §2]** |
| Reef habitat polygons | Low | Allen Coral Atlas covers the whole Red Sea |
| Bathymetry / coastline | Low | GMRT/GEBCO global |
| Ocean currents | Medium | Copernicus global 1/12° covers it, but resolution degrades the further from a confined basin you go — the Gulf of Aqaba caveat becomes *worse*, not better, on the open Red Sea coast |
| Sediment-load proxy | Medium | Needs local land cover/soil re-derivation (Pulga's chain is scripted, so mostly re-run) |
| Validation event | **High** | Each new site needs its own documented event + clear post-event satellite scene. This is the real cost of expansion, and it is the same gate Abd owns for Aqaba. |

**The honest constraint [judgement]:** geography is cheap to change; **validation is not**.
Every new coastline needs its own literature-documented event and its own cloud-free
post-event scene. That is worth saying in the pitch — it shows the team understands what
scaling actually costs.
