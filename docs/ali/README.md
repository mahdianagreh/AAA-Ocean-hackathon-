# Where else does the wadi-to-reef problem happen?

**Global analogue and market scan for ReefShield Aqaba**

**Author:** Ali · **Date:** 2026-08-02 · **Status:** first pass, sources cited inline

---

## Why this document set exists

The concept doc, [`reefshield_aqaba_concept.md`](../../reefshield_aqaba_concept.md) §5.2, tells the
team not to claim global novelty without doing the search:

> The team should **not** claim global uniqueness unless a formal patent and literature
> search is completed.

This is that search, on the market side. It answers three questions a judge will ask:

1. **Is Aqaba a one-off, or a category?** — If it is a one-off, the platform is a local
   tool. If it is a category, it is a product.
2. **Where else does the identical problem occur?** — Named sites, with evidence, ranked
   by how little the platform would have to change.
3. **Has someone already built this?** — Prior art, and precisely where the gap is.

The scan is split into **Part A — MENA** (the home market and the densest cluster of
analogues on Earth) and **Part B — Rest of World** (where the same signature recurs under
different climate drivers).

---

## The three findings that matter

### 1. It is a category, not a one-off — and MENA is the densest cluster on Earth

The physical signature (hyper-arid catchment → ephemeral wadi → episodic sediment pulse →
nearshore reef) repeats along **the entire Red Sea and Gulf of Aqaba rim** and across the
**Gulf of Oman** — that is Part A, and it is essentially one continuous market. Outside
MENA the same signature recurs in **southwest Madagascar**, the **semi-arid Caribbean
islands**, and **arid Baja California**, under different rainfall drivers.

Globally, the World Resources Institute puts **more than a quarter of the world's reefs
under threat from watershed-based pollution, with about 10% highly threatened**
([WRI Reefs at Risk Revisited](https://www.wri.org/research/reefs-risk-revisited)).

The Gulf of Aqaba is unusual even within MENA: **four countries share one basin roughly
15–25 km wide**. A single storm hits Aqaba (Jordan), Eilat (Israel), Taba/Nuweiba (Egypt)
and Haql (Saudi Arabia) simultaneously. ReefShield is therefore inherently regional rather
than national — see [`02-mena-gulf-of-aqaba.md`](02-mena-gulf-of-aqaba.md).

### 2. Aridity alone is not the problem — and this sharpens the pitch

**Ningaloo Reef, Western Australia is arid and has excellent water quality precisely
because it has almost no runoff** ([Australian Heritage
Database](https://www.dcceew.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/ningaloo.pdf)). That is a
genuine counterexample and it forces a more precise problem statement.

The damaging combination is not "arid coast + reef". It is:

> **rare high-intensity rainfall × steep short catchments × a reef within a few kilometres
> of the outlet × a narrow or semi-enclosed receiving basin × development at the outlet ×
> no local monitoring.**

Aqaba scores on all six. Ningaloo scores on two. That distinction is the screening rubric
in [`01-problem-signature.md`](01-problem-signature.md), and stating it out loud is
stronger than claiming every desert coast has this problem.

### 3. The prior art validates the concept and leaves the arid coast empty

- **eReefs** (Australia) does operationally, for the Great Barrier Reef, most of what
  ReefShield proposes — near-real-time hydrodynamic, sediment and water-quality modelling
  with flood-plume tracking. It is the **world's largest reef forecasting programme**, built
  by four national agencies
  ([eReefs](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/1755876X.2019.1650589)). It proves
  the concept is sound and fundable. It also does not transfer: it assumes perennial
  rivers, wet-tropics hydrology, gauged catchments and a decade of national investment.
- **NOAA Coral Reef Watch** monitors thermal stress for 95% of the world's reefs — but its
  only ocean-colour water-clarity product covers **exactly two regions, Hawaii and Puerto
  Rico, and is still experimental**
  ([NOAA CRW](https://coralreefwatch.noaa.gov/satellite/product_overview.php)).
- **Allen Coral Atlas** maps habitat, not risk. **Ridge-to-Reef** programmes plan watershed
  interventions retrospectively; they do not forecast events.

**No operational, forecast-mode, land-to-sea sediment exposure system exists for an
arid or hyper-arid reef coast anywhere in the world.** That is the defensible claim, and it
is narrower and more survivable than "nobody has done this."

Full analysis in [`08-prior-art.md`](08-prior-art.md).

## How to read the evidence markers

This set follows the project's existing source-vs-derived discipline (see
[`docs/event_dates.md`](../event_dates.md) §3). Every substantive claim carries one of:

| Marker | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **[sourced]** | Stated in a cited document. Follow the link. |
| **[derived]** | Computed or reasoned from sourced facts, with the reasoning shown. |
| **[judgement]** | My assessment. Arguable. Labelled so it is never mistaken for data. |
| **[unverified]** | Encountered but not confirmed against a primary source. Do not cite. |

## What I could not verify

Recorded so nobody quotes it by accident.

1. **"~28,000 km of arid/hyper-arid coastline worldwide."** This figure surfaced in a search
   result summary attributed to the flash-flood literature. I fetched the Kalman et al.
   (2025) NHESS paper in full and **the figure is not in it**. The Katz et al. (2015)
   ScienceDirect page returns HTTP 403 and could not be checked. **Do not put this number
   on a slide until someone reads Katz et al. (2015) directly.** If it holds up it is an
   excellent market-size line; unsourced, it is exactly the kind of claim that collapses
   under questioning.
2. **Jordan-specific reef tourism revenue.** Egypt-wide and global figures are sourced
   (see [`10-sources.md`](10-sources.md)); a defensible Aqaba-specific number was not
   found. ASEZA or the Aqaba Marine Reserve Management Plan 2022–2026 is the likely holder.
3. **Whether any shortlisted site already runs an internal, unpublished system.** Only
   published/public systems were searchable.

## What this licenses the team to claim

**Safe [derived]:**

> ReefShield addresses a problem class that recurs along arid reef coasts worldwide —
> more than a quarter of the world's reefs face watershed-based pollution — and no
> operational forecast-mode system for it exists on any arid coast. The nearest working
> analogue, Australia's eReefs, required four national agencies and assumes perennial-river
> hydrology that does not apply here.

**Also safe, and worth saying because it is the honest scope [sourced]:** most of the
world's reefs sit on *wet* tropical coasts — Ningaloo's UNESCO nomination notes that its
proximity to an arid coast "distinguishes it from most reefs in the world, which are found
associated with wet tropical rainforest habitat." Arid reef coasts are the **minority
case**, which is exactly why they have been skipped by the systems built so far. A smaller,
neglected niche is a better story than an inflated one.

**Unsafe — do not say:**

> Nobody has ever modelled sediment plumes / coral risk / flood forecasting.

That is false and a judge will know it. The novelty is the *integration on a data-poor arid
coast*, not the invention of any single component.
