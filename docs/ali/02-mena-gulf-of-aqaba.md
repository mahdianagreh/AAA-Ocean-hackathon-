# 02 · MENA — The Gulf of Aqaba rim

**Part A, Tier 1. The same basin, the same storms, the same reefs, four flags.**

---

## The headline

The Gulf of Aqaba is **14–26 km wide** ([GFCR concept note](https://mptf.undp.org/sites/default/files/documents/2022-12/gulf_of_aqaba_and_n_red_sea_reefs_programme_redacted.pdf)) and about 160 km long. Jordan, Israel, Egypt
and Saudi Arabia all hold coastline on it. **A storm system over the northern Gulf produces
flash floods in all four jurisdictions in the same 48 hours.**

That is not a marketing framing — it is what the October 2016 event actually did. The
event ReefShield is built around is documented from the **Israeli** side (Eilat), by an
Israeli-led team, using an Israeli canal outlet, while the project models it from the
**Jordanian** side. Same storm. Same gulf. Same reefs, which do not observe the border.

**Implication for the product [judgement]:** ReefShield's natural unit is the *basin*, not
the *country*. A four-country basin with no shared operational monitoring is a stronger
pitch than a single-city tool, and it opens multilateral funding that a national tool
cannot reach.

## Site 1 · Eilat, Israel — Score 11/12

**The strongest analogue in the world, because it is literally the same event.** It scores
11 rather than 12 only on C6: Eilat is *better* instrumented than Aqaba — offshore
instruments recorded the 2016 salinity and turbidity response — so it is marginally less
data-poor. Everything else is identical.

| Criterion | Score | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| C1 ephemeral | 2 | Mean rainfall **27 mm/yr**; no perennial rivers; ephemeral rivers discharge only during brief flooding events ([Kalman et al. 2025](https://nhess.copernicus.org/articles/25/3201/2025/)) **[sourced]** |
| C2 intense episodic | 2 | Oct 2016: ≈66 h event, **≈82% of rainfall in 18 h** **[sourced]** |
| C3 reef proximity | 2 | Coral-rich northern Gulf; the 2015 paper's title is literally "the coral-rich Gulf of Aqaba" **[sourced]** |
| C4 confined basin | 2 | Same 14–26 km basin **[sourced]** |
| C5 development | 2 | Eilat city, port, tourism infrastructure **[sourced]** |
| C6 data-poor | 1 | Better instrumented than Aqaba — offshore instruments recorded the 2016 salinity/turbidity response — but still no operational forecast system **[sourced]** |

### What the literature establishes here

- **Sediment mass.** Approximately **24,000 t of suspended sediment entered the sea** during
  the October 2016 event; the February 2013 event moved **21,000 t** to the Gulf of
  Eilat/Aqaba from the Kinnet Canal ([Kalman et al. 2025](https://nhess.copernicus.org/articles/25/3201/2025/))
  **[sourced]**. Both **substantially exceeded annual atmospheric dust input** — which is
  the single most quotable fact in this whole scan.
- **Mechanism.** Desert flash floods form **hyperpycnal flows** — sediment-dense water that
  sinks and travels along the seabed rather than floating as a surface plume — in the
  coral-rich Gulf of Aqaba, despite the Red Sea's high salinity ([Katz et al.
  2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0012821X15001119))
  **[sourced]**. This is the caveat [`tasks/nizar.md`](../../tasks/nizar.md) already flags:
  surface currents may be the wrong forcing for part of the sediment mass.
- **Generalisability, stated by the authors themselves.** Kalman et al. explicitly frame
  their work as a template: *"Developing a framework that traces the entire sequence of
  events during a single flood can serve as a model for understanding similar processes in
  other arid coastal systems"* **[sourced, verified verbatim]**. **That sentence is the
  scientific licence for this entire document set** — the paper the project is built on says
  the approach is meant to generalise.
- **The paper already treats this as a two-country event.** Verbatim: *"In the following
  18 h however, both sides of the Arava Valley (Eilat and **Wadi Yutum** catchments)
  experienced intense storms, adding 109 million m³ water (∼82% of the total)."*
  **[sourced, verified verbatim]**
- **Rainfall intensity, in context.** Verbatim: *"In Eilat (mean annual rainfall = 27 mm,
  SD = 21 mm), **128% of the average fell within hours**."* **[sourced, verified verbatim]**
  More than a year's rain in hours is the single most vivid line available for the pitch.

> ### ⚠️ Two corrections this implies for the project's own docs
>
> 1. **The 82% / 18 h figure is about water volume across *both* the Eilat and Wadi Yutum
>    catchments — 109 million m³ — not rainfall depth over the Aqaba box alone.**
>    [`docs/event_dates.md`](../event_dates.md) currently records it as *"≈82% of total
>    rainfall fell during a concentrated 18-hour spell"*, which is a fair paraphrase but
>    loses the volume basis and the two-catchment scope. Worth tightening before it reaches

> 2. **The paper explicitly names Wadi Yutum**, which is Mahdi's `AQ-C01`. That is direct

### Transboundary machinery that already exists

- A **Memorandum of Understanding between Israel and Jordan** covers coral reef mapping,
  monitoring and data management, with both states confirming intent to cooperate on
  ecosystem monitoring in the northern Gulf ([ICRI](https://icriforum.org/countries-join-efforts-for-red-sea-corals-aqaba/))
  **[sourced]**.
- **Coral health monitoring stations** were installed in Eilat (late 2021) and Aqaba, as
  part of a network intended to cover key Red Sea locations **[sourced]**.
- The **Marine Science Station in Aqaba** and the **InterUniversity Institute in Eilat**
  already run joint surveys **[sourced]**.

**Why this matters commercially [judgement]:** there is an existing cooperation framework
with named institutions on both sides, and it currently covers *monitoring* — not
*forecasting*. ReefShield slots into a gap inside an agreement that already exists, rather
than requiring a new one.

## Site 2 · Sinai — Taba, Nuweiba, Dahab (Egypt) — Score 11/12

**Same gulf, western shore, and the single largest wadi catchment on it.**

| Criterion | Score | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| C1 ephemeral | 2 | Classic Sinai wadi systems |
| C2 intense episodic | 2 | **17–18 January 2010** flash floods battered the Sinai Peninsula; six deaths, dozens wounded ([Geomatics, Natural Hazards and Risk](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/19475705.2012.731657)) **[sourced]** |
| C3 reef proximity | 2 | Dahab and Nuweiba fringing reefs are world-class dive sites, metres from shore **[sourced]** |
| C4 confined basin | 2 | Same Gulf of Aqaba |
| C5 development | 2 | Nuweiba port and ferry terminal; Dahab and Taba tourism |
| C6 data-poor | 1 | Flash-flood *hazard* mapping exists; nothing marine-facing |

**Wadi Watir → Nuweiba** is the standout. Wadi Watir is the outlet that discharges directly
into the city of Nuweiba, and the greater Nuweiba area has been assessed for flash-flood
risk along it **[sourced]**. Sinai's very-high-sensitivity flash-flood zone comprises six
major basins including **wadis Dahab and Watir**
([Water, MDPI](https://doi.org/10.3390/w14152434)) **[sourced]**.

There is even prior work on **forecasting flash floods over Wadi Watir using the WRF
model** **[sourced]** — i.e. someone has already done the *atmospheric* half on the Egyptian
side, with no marine continuation. That is precisely ReefShield's differentiator.

**What would need to change:** almost nothing architecturally. New AOI, new catchment
delineation, Egyptian reef habitat from Allen Coral Atlas. The IMERG/ERA5 ingestion,
antecedent features, and plume engine are unchanged **[judgement]**.

## Site 3 · Saudi Gulf of Aqaba coast + NEOM — Score 11/12

**The best-funded version of the same problem, and it is being built right now.**

| Criterion | Score | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| C1 ephemeral | 2 | Northern Hijaz wadi systems |
| C2 intense episodic | 2 | Torrential rains in 2024 caused widespread disruption across Arabia; sediment-loaded plumes discharged by flash floods deposit onto the seabed **[sourced]** |
| C3 reef proximity | 2 | UNESCO tentative listing: *Coral Reefs of the Gulf of Aqaba and the Red Sea in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia* **[sourced]** |
| C4 confined basin | 2 | Same Gulf |
| C5 development | 2 | **NEOM** under construction directly on this coast |
| C6 data-poor | 1 | New baselines being established; no public operational system |

The **NEOM brine pools** are positioned to receive sediments shed from the coastal zone and
have been identified as an archive of historical tsunamis, **flash floods** and seismicity
in the Gulf of Aqaba **[sourced]** — independent confirmation that flash-flood sediment
reaches and accumulates in this basin.

Saudi Arabia is running **three giga-projects — NEOM, Red Sea Global and Jeddah Central —**
as part of a **$1.3 trillion economic expansion plan**, and researchers note that
*understanding the health status of coral reefs is paramount to developing baselines and
management strategies to minimise human impact* given this development
([AGBI](https://www.agbi.com/analysis/sustainability/2024/12/red-sea-coral-to-get-extra-protection-from-tourists/))
**[sourced]**.

**Commercial read [judgement]:** this is the highest-budget, highest-urgency version of the
exact problem, on the same body of water, ~100 km from Aqaba. A working demo built in
Jordan is directly demonstrable to a Saudi buyer. Of every site in this scan, this is the
one with real money attached.

## What the Gulf of Aqaba cluster means for the pitch

**[derived]** Four adjacent sites, all Tier 1, all sharing one 14–26 km basin, with:

- a documented event affecting all of them in the same 48 hours,
- an existing bilateral monitoring MoU that covers monitoring but **not** forecasting,
- a $1.3T development programme on one shore,
- and no operational land-to-sea forecasting system on any of them.

The honest framing for a judge:

> "We built this for Aqaba. But the storm that generated our demo event flooded four
> countries at once, and the paper we validate against was written from the other side of
> the border. The gulf is 15 km wide. Nobody can solve this one coastline at a time."
